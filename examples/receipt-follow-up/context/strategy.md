## Positioning

**Who this product is for:** the person who owns spend control in a 50–250 person company and is
usually not a finance department — a finance lead, a deputy CFO, or an ops manager who inherited
the company cards.

**What they are like:** they close the month in under five working days, carry the accountant
relationship personally, have 20–60 cardholders and no authority over any of them, and already
keep at least one spreadsheet outside the product.

**What frustrates them:** work that lands on their desk because nobody else will do it — chasing,
re-categorising, re-explaining policy. They do not complain about features. They complain about
hours.

**Who this product is not for:** companies with a procurement function and a formal purchase
approval chain. That buyer wants a control system; ours wants their evenings back.

## Goal for 2026

Be the tool the finance lead refuses to give up at renewal — measured as net revenue retention,
not new logos.

| Parameter | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Net revenue retention | 108% by Dec 2026 (currently 96%) |
| Self-reported admin hours per month-end close | −40% against the Mar 2026 benchmark of 6.5h |

## Product principles

1. **Automate the chase, not the person.** The system does the following-up. We ship nothing whose
   primary output is a ranked list of colleagues who are behind, however often an admin asks.
2. **The admin's hour is the unit of value.** A change that removes no minutes from the person who
   owns the close is a nice-to-have.
3. **No new habits for cardholders.** They did not choose us. A change that asks them to learn
   something pays for itself only if it also removes an existing step.
4. **Boring where money is.** Ledger, export, and reconciliation correctness beat everything above
   when they conflict.

## Explicit trade-offs

**We are:** opinionated about defaults, building for the single-admin workspace first, and willing
to be worse than competitors at breadth of card coverage.

**We are not:** a procurement or purchase-approval system, an HR-adjacent compliance reporting
tool, or a product that competes on integration count.

## Priorities for H2 2026

| Band | Items |
| --- | --- |
| **MUST** | Remove manual work from the month-end close for the single-admin workspace; accounting export correctness |
| **SHOULD** | Mobile receipt capture reliability; bulk category editing |
| **COULD** | Project and cost-centre tagging |
| **WON'T** | Pre-approval and spend-authorisation workflows — that is the procurement buyer we declined in Positioning. Third-party travel-card ingestion — the data partnership is unsigned and we will not fake it with statement uploads. Employee compliance scoring or ranking of any kind. |

## Quality bar

A change ships when it removes a step from the admin's close without adding one for a cardholder,
and we can show the removed step in a before-and-after measurement on a real workspace segment.

## What every PRD must answer

- How does this serve the customer named in Positioning?
- How does it drive net revenue retention — directly, or through a named strategic effect?
- Which priority band does it belong to, and does it fit H2 2026?
- Which product principle does it advance, and which one does it put under pressure?
- Which explicit trade-off does it approach or cross?

A PRD that cannot answer these is not blocked from existing. It is blocked from claiming
strategic fit.
